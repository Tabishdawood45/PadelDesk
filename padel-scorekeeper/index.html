Padel scoring, explained in 60 seconds

Padel borrows tennis scoring: points climb 0 → 15 → 30 → 40 → game. Win 6 games with a two-game lead and you take the set; at 6-6 you play a tiebreak to 7 (win by 2) and the set ends 7-6. Matches are normally best of three sets.

The only part people argue about is what happens at 40-40 (deuce) — and since January 2026 there are three official answers. Agree on one before the match starts, or just pick it in the counter above:

ModeWhat happens at 40-40Used where
AdvantageWin two points in a row to take the game — can repeat foreverClassic/club play, long matches
Golden PointOne single point decides the game immediatelyFast social play; former pro standard
Star PointUp to two advantage cycles; still level after the second return to deuce → one deciding point2026 FIP rulebook; Premier Padel & CUPRA FIP Tour from 2026

The Star Point was approved by the FIP General Assembly in late 2025 and came into force on 1 January 2026 as a middle ground between endless advantage battles and the all-or-nothing Golden Point. Our counter tracks the cycles for you and flashes “STAR POINT” when the deciding point arrives — read the full breakdown in The Star Point Rule in Padel.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the counter say "2 advantage cycles left"?
That's Star Point mode doing its job. Each time a team wins advantage but loses the next point, the game returns to deuce — that's one cycle used. After two cycles, the next point is the deciding Star Point.
Can I use this for a normal tennis-style game?
Yes — choose Advantage mode and it behaves exactly like traditional scoring with unlimited deuces.
I tapped the wrong side — what now?
Hit Undo. Every point is stored on your device, so you can step back as many points as you need.
Does it work without internet?
Once the page is loaded it runs entirely on your phone — no connection needed during the match.
