How to choose a padel racket (the 4 things that matter)

1. Shape = where the power lives

ShapeSweet spotBalanceBest for
RoundLarge, centralLow (head-light)Beginners, control players, anyone protecting an arm
TeardropMedium, slightly highMediumImproving club players — the safe all-round pick
DiamondSmaller, highHigh (head-heavy)Advanced attackers with clean technique

2. Weight & balance = your arm's best friend (or worst enemy)

Most adult rackets sit around 345–375 g. Grams matter less than where the mass sits: a head-heavy 360 g racket swings "heavier" than a head-light 370 g one. If you've ever felt elbow pain, go lighter and lower-balanced — padel elbow ruins more seasons than any bad backhand.

3. Foam & face = feel

Soft EVA or FOAM cores are comfortable and forgiving; hard EVA gives feedback and pace to fast swings. Fibreglass faces are softer and cheaper; carbon faces are stiffer, more precise and more durable.

4. Price = honesty about your level

A €300 diamond racket does not make anyone a better player — in most club games it adds errors. Buy the racket for the player you are this season, not the one in your head. Entry and mid-range rackets from the big brands are excellent in 2026.

Frequently asked questions

Which racket should a complete beginner buy?
A round, light-ish racket with soft foam from any major brand's entry line. Spend the savings on a coaching session — it returns far more than carbon.
When should I move from round to teardrop or diamond?
When your positioning and preparation are automatic and you're consistently finishing points at the net — typically after a season or two of regular play. Move one step at a time: round → teardrop → (maybe) diamond.
How often should I replace a racket?
Club players typically get 1–2 seasons; the foam loses response before the frame cracks. Sun, heat (car boots!) and wet courts shorten the life considerably.
