How a Mexicano works

A Mexicano starts with one random round. After that, the generator ranks everyone by points and builds the next round from the standings: on court 1, the 1st and 3rd-placed players partner against 2nd and 4th; on court 2, 5th and 7th against 6th and 8th; and so on. Winners climb toward court 1, so the top court's final round becomes a de-facto final.

The effect: matches get tighter every round and players settle at their level. It's the best format when abilities vary a lot — the single best cure for the "someone wins 8-0 and nobody enjoyed it" problem.

Quick answers

How many players for a Mexicano?
At least 4, up to 40. It's cleanest in multiples of four, but the generator handles odd counts by rotating sit-outs fairly.
Does the first round matter if it's random?
It just seeds the ranking. If you give players a level, the generator can seed round one by level instead of at random, so the very first matches are balanced too.
Can resting players still rank fairly?
Yes — resters can be credited the round average, so sitting out once doesn't wreck their standing.
