What a padel court really costs in 2026

Headline "court prices" are misleading because the court structure is only part of the project. A complete build has four big line items:

  • Court system — steel frame, 10–12mm glass walls, mesh, turf and sand. This is the price most suppliers quote.
  • Groundwork — a reinforced concrete ring beam and base with drainage, designed for local wind loads. Often 35–45% of the whole budget and the item first-time buyers forget.
  • Lighting — LED floodlights if you want evening play (where most club revenue is).
  • Cover — a canopy or full building. In rainy markets like the UK this is what makes a court bookable year-round; it can cost more than the court itself.

Five things that move your quote

  1. Ground conditions. Building on an old tennis court can save £10,000+ per court versus virgin ground that needs excavation and drainage.
  2. Wind and corrosion. UK guidance requires structures engineered for local wind loads and better corrosion protection than continental Europe — cheap imported courts can be a false economy.
  3. Multiple courts. Site work, access and planning are largely fixed costs, so the per-court price drops when you build two or more at once.
  4. Planning and noise. Court lighting and ball-strike noise are the common objections. Budget time (and sometimes acoustic fencing) for approval.
  5. Maintenance. Plan £1,500–£3,000 per court per year for cleaning and minor repairs, and a sinking fund for turf replacement every 5–10 years.

Is a court a good investment?

Demand is running ahead of supply in all three markets: Britain passed one million active players in 2026 with court occupancy around 85% at peak and roughly half of UK players reporting difficulty booking peak slots; France added more than 1,000 courts in a year and passed 5,000; the US crossed 1,000 courts with padel now in 37 states. That's why planning applications for UK padel courts in the first half of 2026 already more than doubled all of 2025. None of this guarantees returns — location, cover and operations decide — but the underlying demand is real.

Frequently asked questions

Can I build a padel court at home?
If you have roughly 11×21 metres of accessible flat space, yes. UK home builds typically run £25,000–£80,000 depending on kit quality and groundwork. Check our calculator with "existing base" if you have a tennis court to convert, and confirm planning rules first.
How long does a build take?
The court installation itself takes days to a few weeks; groundwork, planning approval and (for indoor) the building are what stretch projects to months. Modular buildings can go up in as little as 4 weeks once approved.
How big is a padel court?
The playing area is 10×20 m. With structure and run-off you should plan around 11×21 m per court, plus access and circulation space.
Why is the US range so wide?
The American market is young: courts and glass are mostly imported, freight and state-level construction costs differ hugely, and few published benchmarks exist yet. Treat any single number sceptically and collect at least three quotes.
