The Star Point rule in padel, explained

The Star Point is padel's biggest scoring change in years. Added to the official FIP rulebook from 1 January 2026, it sits between the endless classic Advantage and the all-or-nothing Golden Point.

How it works

  1. The game reaches 40-40 (deuce). Advantage is played as normal.
  2. A pair wins advantage but loses the next point → back to deuce. That's one cycle.
  3. It happens again — another advantage lost, back to deuce. Two cycles used.
  4. Now one single point decides the game: the Star Point. Winner takes the game, whoever they are.

So unlike the Golden Point (sudden death at the first deuce), the Star Point only arrives after both sides have had real chances to close the game. In mixed doubles there's a notable detail: on the Star Point, the receiver must be the same gender as the server.

Where it came from

The change was approved unanimously by the FIP General Assembly on 28 November 2025 and entered the rulebook on 1 January 2026. From the 2026 season it's used across the professional and federation game — Premier Padel, the CUPRA FIP Tour, FIP Promises and FIP Beyond — and USA Padel adopted it for sanctioned tournaments in place of the Golden Point. The logic: the Golden Point was criticised for deciding games on a single coin-flip point, while classic advantage battles could drag matches past broadcast windows. The Star Point caps the length of a game while keeping some of the back-and-forth drama.

Whichever you play, agree on it before the match — it's the number one cause of on-court arguments at deuce.
