Americano vs Mexicano: what's the actual difference?

Both are social padel formats with rotating partners and individual points. The difference is one thing: how the next round is paired — and that one thing changes the whole feel of the night.

Side by side

AmericanoMexicano
ScheduleFully fixed before round 1Re-calculated every round
Pairing logicEveryone partners everyone (rotation)By live ranking: 1st & 3rd vs 2nd & 4th on court 1, and so on
Match closenessRandom — blowouts happenGets closer every round
Best forEven-ish groups, maximum social mixingMixed levels, competitive-but-fair nights
Meet everyone?Yes — that's the pointNo — you gravitate to your level
SuspenseStandings can swing lateTop court final round is a de-facto final

How a Mexicano round is built

After each round the standings update. The generator then sends 1st and 3rd vs 2nd and 4th to court 1, 5th and 7th vs 6th and 8th to court 2, and so on. Winners climb toward court 1, and the last round on the top court is effectively the final. (This is exactly how our generator pairs Mexicano rounds.)

Which should your group play tonight?

  • Everyone roughly the same level? Americano. Maximum variety, everyone meets.
  • Beginners mixed with strong players? Mexicano. By round 3 everyone is in a close match — the single best cure for the "8–0 and nobody had fun" problem.
  • People came with partners? Team Americano (fixed pairs, round robin).
  • Want mixed pairs? Mixed Americano — every pair one man + one woman.

Still undecided? Run Americano for 8 players or fewer (the rotation maths is beautiful at 8 — see why), Mexicano for bigger or uneven groups.
