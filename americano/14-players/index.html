The sit-out maths

Three courts seat 12, so 2 of your 14 rest each round. After 7 rounds everyone has rested exactly once (2 × 7 = 14). That makes 7 (or 14) rounds the fair counts. The generator keeps rests even, avoids back-to-back benching, shows who's next, and can credit resters the round average.

Only have 2 courts?

Then 8 play and 6 rest each round — a lot of sitting. With 14 players, three courts is worth booking. If you can only get two, consider splitting into a 6 and an 8 and running two separate Americanos.

Timing

7 rounds of 21 points ≈ 100 minutes. For a 2-hour slot, 7 rounds of 24 or 8 of 16 works well.

Quick answers

How many rounds for 14 padel players?
Seven rounds on 3 courts means everyone rests exactly once. Play 7 or 14 for perfectly even rests; 6–8 is the realistic sweet spot.
How many courts do 14 players need?
Three is ideal — 12 play, 2 rest each round. Two courts leaves 6 resting each round, which is a lot of standing around.
What if a 15th or 16th player arrives?
Add them mid-session and the generator rebalances. At 16 you reach a clean setup with no sit-outs on 4 courts — see the 16-player page.
